Tuning

Give every setup change a job.

Build a baseline, describe the problem precisely, test one meaningful variable and keep notes about the result.

Start with a baseline

Drive the same section several times before changing anything. Record the behavior you want to improve rather than simply deciding that the setup feels wrong.

Turn a feeling into a question

Ask whether the car rotates too slowly, becomes unstable under throttle, responds too sharply to steering, or struggles to maintain a useful exit. A specific question is easier to test.

Change one variable

Make one meaningful adjustment and repeat the same section. Changing suspension, steering and power together may produce a different car, but it does not reveal which change caused the result.

StageWhat to record
BaselineCurrent behavior and repeatability.
ChangeThe single adjustment.
TestSeveral runs on the same section.
DecisionKeep the change or revert it.
Reverting is useful. It prevents a poor adjustment from becoming part of a confusing setup.

Know when to stop

If your driving inputs are inconsistent, more tuning may hide the real issue. Return to practice, stabilize the technique and test the setup again.
