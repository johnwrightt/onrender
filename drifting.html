Drifting

Control the slide before chasing maximum angle.

Practice initiation, steering corrections, throttle balance and transitions as separate skills before combining them.

Make initiation repeatable

Choose a simple corner and a visual point where you want the slide to begin. Repeating that point gives you a stable starting condition for the rest of the drift.

Manage angle

Large steering corrections can create a chain of overcorrections. Watch the direction of the car and use smaller changes when possible. The goal is enough control to anticipate the next movement.

Use throttle deliberately

Throttle changes can affect rear rotation. If the car is rotating farther than intended, reducing throttle may help settle it. If the slide is fading, a controlled increase can change the balance. Different cars and setups respond differently, so test rather than assuming one setting works everywhere.

Practice transitions separately

A transition becomes easier when the first drift finishes in a predictable state. Practice changing direction without trying to maximize angle. Smooth timing gives you a better starting point for the next movement.

A useful drift run is explainable: where you initiated, what controlled the angle, and what caused the transition to succeed or fail.
