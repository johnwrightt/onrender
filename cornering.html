Cornering

Turn each corner into three repeatable decisions.

Use reference points, controlled entry speed, deliberate rotation and clean exits to make cornering easier to diagnose.

Entry

Choose a visible reference point for slowing or changing your approach. A road marking, barrier, sign or building edge can work. The best reference is one you can recognize repeatedly.

If entry speed and position change every run, later feedback becomes difficult to interpret. Make the starting condition consistent first.

Rotation

If the car runs wide, adding steering is not automatically the answer. The entry may have been too fast, or the steering may have been applied too abruptly. Change one part of the approach and observe what happens.

Exit

The current corner affects the next section. A wide or poorly positioned exit can make the next input harder. Think beyond the apex and leave yourself a usable path for acceleration and positioning.

Five-run drill: keep the setup unchanged and concentrate only on entering the same corner from the same position.

Describe the mistake

Replace “the car feels bad” with something specific such as “the front runs wide after throttle is added near the exit.” Specific observations create useful tests.
